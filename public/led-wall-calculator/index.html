How it works

What the numbers mean.

Resolution and size

Each panel has a fixed number of pixels, so the wall's resolution is pixels per panel times columns and rows. Its size is the panel size times the same. Pixel pitch is the panel width divided by its pixels.

Outputs

"Straight" counts how many 1920 × 1080 or 3840 × 2160 outputs the wall needs if you cut it into output-sized tiles. Most processors can also fold a long wall into one output, so the pixel count gives the least it could take.

Processor ports

A 1 Gb Ethernet port on an LED processor carries about 650,000 pixels at 60 Hz in 8-bit colour (about 780,000 at 50 Hz). Panels can't be split across ports, so the count goes by whole panels. 10-bit, high frame rates and HDR carry fewer.

Power and circuits

Max power is every pixel at full white; typical is what the maker quotes for normal video, usually a third to half of max. Plan circuits on max. Each circuit is loaded to 80% of its breaker: 20 A at 120 V and 208 V, 16 A at 230 V.

Weight

Panels only. Add frames, rigging, ground stack or flown hardware, cable and the processor from your rental house's numbers.

Viewing distance

A rule of thumb: the nearest an audience should sit is about the pixel pitch in millimetres as metres (2.6 mm, about 2.6 m), and a comfortable distance is about three times that. Cameras have their own rules for moiré.

Where do the panel figures come from?

Pixels and size are from each maker's spec page. Power and weight are from the manufacturer spec too, but rental houses use different versions and revisions, so check your rental house's sheet before you order power. Choose Custom panel to type in your own.

Why doesn't my wall fit a 4K output when the pixel count is under 8.3 million?

An output is 3840 pixels wide. A wall wider than that has to be folded or split into several outputs, even if the total pixels would fit. The calculator shows both the straight count and the least it could take.

What is a pixel map for?

You load it full screen on the output that feeds the processor. If every panel shows its own number in the right place, the processor's mapping matches the wall. The arrows show the data path so the crew can check the cabling against it.

The pixel map won't download for a very big wall.

Browsers limit the size of an image they can draw. Most desktop browsers manage up to about 16,000 pixels on a side; iPhones and iPads give up at about 16 million pixels in total. Use a desktop browser for big walls, or make the map in sections.
