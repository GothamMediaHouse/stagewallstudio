How it works

Where the traffic goes.

Each viewer is its own stream

NDI sends a separate copy of a source to every computer that takes it, unless multicast is turned on. Three computers watching one camera means three streams leave that camera.

Full quality or preview

Every NDI source also offers a small preview stream at 640 × 360. Multiviewers use it for small tiles, so they take far less than a switcher or recorder does.

The links that matter

On a switched network each computer has its own port. What counts is the port of the busiest computer, the port of each source, and the link between switches if the show spans more than one.

The 75% line

NDI traffic comes in bursts, so plan to fill no more than about three quarters of a link. The meters mark that line. Past it, frames start to drop before the link is truly full.

Wi-Fi and remote viewers

Full-quality NDI over Wi-Fi rarely holds up. For a viewer on another network, send NDI HX or use NDI Bridge, and check the upload speed at the studio.

The figures

The numbers are the maximum bit rates NDI publishes in its white paper. Real streams are often lower, especially for still or simple pictures, so these are safe planning numbers.
